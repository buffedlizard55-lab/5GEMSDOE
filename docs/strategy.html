3. What they prove

  1. The best file is a DISCOVERY field, not a catalogue re-tracer: 78% of its 172,974 pixels lie >3 px from any supplied fault and only 3.7% sit on masked pixels. The 0.1563 was earned off-catalogue.
  2. Adding 9,430 unmasked pixels (10,668 in total; a strict superset) moved the score by -0.0003: post-hoc unions of existing fields are at break-even. More pixels from the SAME detectors do not raise the score; a better detector does.
  3. Spaced nodes vs dense ridge, same model and budget: 0.1193 vs 0.1152 (+0.0041). GEMSDOE3's local proxy (its README, audit fold) predicted +0.0924 for the same contrast. The local proxies over-reward sparsity by ~23×; the emission geometry is a second-order effect on the real board.
  4. Training on catalogue-gap (SGMC) pixels only scored 0.0830 vs 0.1193 for the same architecture with the union target: the SGMC gap population does NOT resemble the expert new-fault set. Any strategy that leans on it as truth should be down-weighted.
  5. CNN ensemble (0.1563) beats the pixel-wise HGB model (0.1152–0.1193) at similar budgets with almost disjoint supports (Jaccard 0.066): spatial context is worth ~+0.04, and the two families find different things. Their union is bounded between 'no new truth covered' and 'all TP disjoint' — the marginal rule puts the HGB pixels near break-even, so the union is a coin-flip, not a plan.
  6. Every scored file emits ~3–3.6 % of the valid area and lands at 0.08–0.16; the leader is at 0.3049. At DTI 0.30 the break-even marginal hit-rate is 6.5 % (vs 3.3 % at 0.156): the leader's detector finds roughly twice the truth per emitted pixel. The gap is detection quality, not shaping.
  7. The 19 supplied bands contain NO radiometric channel and only a 100 m detrended-elevation pair; the GeoDAWN release itself ships radiometric + magnetic GeoTIFF grids (22103_area1/area2_tiffs.zip) and the organizers hand out 1 m lidar links. Quaternary faults are mapped by experts from scarps in high-resolution topography; that channel is the one this family has never trained on (docs/STRATEGY.md §4).

4. What the metric forces

DTI = T / (0.2·(T+F) + 0.8·G) because TPw + FNw = |G| exactly.

Binary is optimal: For a fixed support, scaling all values by c>0 changes the score to cT/(0.2c(T+F)+0.8G), which is increasing in c; so every emitted pixel should be 1.0 and the whole problem is WHICH pixels to emit.

Marginal-inclusion rule: Under DTI = T/(0.2(T+F)+0.8G), adding a binary pixel with expected TP gain dT and FP cost dF (≤1) raises the score iff dT > 0.2*DTI*dF/(1-0.2*DTI).

5. The density probe (first upload from this site)

A blanket p = 1 on all 5,106,385 unmasked valid pixels covers every truth pixel with k = 1, so its public score D inverts to the hidden truth size: |G| = 0.2·N·D / (1 − D + 0.2·κ·D) (κ ≈ 2–3, the kernel mass per truth pixel; the inversion barely depends on it). Its own score is low by design; it buys the one number every other decision needs. Build: python scripts/density_probe.py · invert: python scripts/density_probe.py --invert SCORE.

6. Hypotheses under test

  • H1 — sub-pixel scarp statistics (primary): 10 m slope / curvature / aspect-coherence / hillshade-contrast from the public 3DEP seamless 1/3″ DEM (record), aggregated to 9 statistics per 100 m cell (scripts/build_topo_features.py, tested on synthetic scarps), stacked with zero resampling via data.aux_feature_paths (configs/config_topo.yaml). Anchor: lidar-trained detectors in Nevada found faults later confirmed by experts on lidar (Hermant et al. 2025).
    Whole-region channel built on a runner: 105 chunks, coverage 100.0% of the footprint, 874.2 s (2026-09-25T19:23:53Z).
  • H2 — GeoDAWN radiometrics (K, eTh, eU, TC): absent from the 19 bands, present as GeoTIFF grids in the USGS release (area1 43.6 MB, area2 230.5 MB). Next session.
  • H3 — directional catalogue continuation: extend traces along strike only where the detector fires (staff: new faults include continuations/splays). Priced by the marginal rule once |G| is known.
  • H4 — budget by the rule, not by a floor: emit in decreasing expected-hit-rate order until the marginal rate crosses 0.2·DTI/(1−0.2·DTI).
  • H5 — Phase-2 leverage: distinct, defensible geometry is worth more after expert review than consensus pixels.

8. Fitting the hidden truth from the five scores — and what it cannot tell us

The board is the only instrument that has ever measured the scored population, so scripts/fit_hidden_prior.py uses it directly: model the expected truth density as a non-negative combination of spatial covariates and use the metric's algebra (DTI = T / (0.2*E + 0.8*G)) to turn each scored file into one linear equation in the weights. 9. A prior from the geothermal literature, not from the data (H6)

Forty years of Great Basin exploration is unusually quantitative about geometry. Faulds & Hinz (World Geothermal Congress 2015, OSTI 1724082; dataset GDR 355, DOI 10.15121/1148722) catalogued the structural setting of ~250 geothermal fields: step-overs / relay ramps ~32%, normal-fault terminations 25%, fault intersections 22%, accommodation zones 9%, displacement transfer zones 5%, pull-aparts 3%, bends 2%, major range-front faults 1%. They also report that Quaternary faults lie within or near most systems, that systems are rare along major range-front faults (clay gouge, stress release), and that tips horse-tail into a myriad of closely-spaced faults — short unmapped strands, which is what the scored set is made of.

scripts/build_structural_targets.py derives those settings from the catalogue's own geometry (no label beyond labels.tif, no score, no model): 6,938 terminations, 2,633 intersections, 1,558 bends and 5,432 relay ramps (111,579 px of ramp) over 59,209 skeleton px, weighted by the published frequencies and smoothed at sigma = 2.0 px. Accommodation zones, transfer zones and pull-aparts need dip, slip sense and strain partitioning, which a trace raster does not carry: they are reported as absent rather than invented.
